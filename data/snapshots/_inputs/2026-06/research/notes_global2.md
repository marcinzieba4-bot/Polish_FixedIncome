# Global PIT facts 15-29 Jun 2026: working notes
Windows (Warsaw publication time, CEST throughout): R0616 = 15 Jun; R0617 = 16 Jun; R0618 = 17 Jun; R0619 = 18 Jun; R0622 = 19-21 Jun (Fri 19 Jun is Juneteenth, US markets closed; weekend); R0623 = 22 Jun; R0624 = 23 Jun; R0625 = 24 Jun; R0626 = 25 Jun; R0629 = 26-28 Jun; R0630 = 29 Jun.
Output: facts.json (156 facts + 17 fed_pricing rows, 24 cb_decisions, 14 cpi rows), built by build/make_json.py from build/f_MMDD.py (facts F and Fed-pricing rows FP per R), build/extra.py (pricing objects, CB decisions, CPI) and build/digest.py. Schema = the May facts_global_2.json:
- facts: published / window_report_date / topic / text / src. fed_pricing rows: published / text / src / window_report_date.
- fed_pricing_by_R, ecb_pricing, boe_pricing: dicts keyed by R, each a short text naming its source date.
- cb_decisions: {bank, date, decision, rate, vote, published, note, src}. It includes the 11 Jun ECB hike and the last pre-window decision of the Fed, BoE, BoJ, RBA, Riksbank, Norges Bank, CNB, NBR, PBoC, MNB, RBNZ and BoC. Filter on published <= the info cut-off. Notes contain only what was known when the decision was published.
- cpi: {country, period, value, core, published, note, src}: EA, DE, ES, US (CPI and PCE), UK, JP, CZ, HU, RO, with pre-window May prints carried at their original release times.
- digest (<= 450 characters) and glob (<= 60 characters, leading space): dicts keyed by R.
The build checks that every cited PAP, Odaily and TE item was downloaded (carried May-file sources are exempt), that the cited item's first-publication time (TE: effective time) falls in the fact's window (no mismatches remain), the key sets of the dicts and the length limits. scripts/schema_check.py compares keys, key order and value types with the May template: SCHEMA OK.
Topics as in May; tariffs are filed under "geopolitics", CEE politics and yield tables under "sovereign", CZ data under "CNB", HU under "MNB".

## Big picture (as sourced, no hindsight)
The preliminary US-Iran deal (MoU) announced on Sunday 14 Jun (Part 1 window) set the fortnight's tone: Hormuz reopening, oil back to pre-war levels, then a fragile truce tested by attacks.
- 15-16 Jun: Brent August -5% to ~$83, then below $80 (lowest since early March); TTF ~EUR42; MS and GS cut forecasts. Iran said Hormuz stays shut until the signing and it will charge for "services"; the MoU was signed electronically. BoJ hiked to 1.0% (7-1); RBA held. ECB pricing fell to ~30bp (one more hike), BoE to one hike by December, Fed December-hike odds to ~56-70%.
- 17 Jun: FOMC hold (12-0), Warsh's first meeting, a terse statement ("will deliver price stability"); SEP median 3.8% for end-2026 (9 of 18 see at least one hike), PCE 3.6%; a year-end hike became fully priced; UST2 +16bp to 4.21%, DXY >100.3. UK May CPI 2.8% (3.0% expected); EZ HICP 3.2% confirmed (core 2.6%).
- 18 Jun: the MoU took effect after Trump (Versailles) and Pezeshkian signed; Khamenei approved; tankers crossed Hormuz. BoE 7-2 hold (Greene, Pill for +25bp); CNB +25bp to 3.75% (6-1), its first hike since 2022; SNB, Norges (hawkish) held. EUR/USD below 1.15.
- 19-21 Jun: the 19 Jun Burgenstock talks were postponed (Lebanon); Burnham won the Makerfield by-election; a September Fed hike was fully priced (Jin10); Wunsch floated a July ECB hike. Sat 20 Jun: the IRGC declared Hormuz closed (US saw no closure); Netanyahu ordered a Lebanon ceasefire; talks opened on Sunday.
- 22-23 Jun: an 18-hour round produced a 60-day roadmap and a Hormuz mechanism; a 60-day US licence for Iranian oil; Starmer announced his resignation (Burnham favourite). Weak European flash PMIs (EZ 49.5, DE 48.0, UK 49.4), strong US (52.2). Lagarde: no need to respond more aggressively. MNB cut to 6.00%. FedWatch ~68% for September; DXY 101.3; tech sell-off.
- 24-25 Jun: Brent below $74 (pre-war levels, contango), gold below $4,000; UST10 4.40%, Bund 2.87%, gilt 10Y a two-month low; US May PCE 4.1% (core 3.4%) in line; Q1 GDP 2.1%; EU states gave final approval to the EU-US tariff deal before the 4 July deadline; a projectile hit a ship off Oman.
- 26-28 Jun: Brent ~$72; US strikes on Iranian missile/drone sites (26-27 Jun) after the Ever Lovely attack; Iran fired at US bases in Kuwait and Bahrain; Trump threatened 100% tariffs on countries with digital taxes; on Sunday the sides agreed to stop attacks and meet in Doha on Tuesday.
- 29 Jun: Brent ~$73; Doha meeting disputed by Iran; Supreme Court kept Fed Governor Cook in post for now; UST10 below 4.38%; Sintra Forum and Thursday payrolls ahead.

## Caveats / conflicts
- **PAP misreads the SEP**: its 17-18 Jun pieces say the median shows "no change" at end-2026 (3.75%), no change in 2027 and one cut in 2028. Official: median 3.8% (the average of 3.625 and 3.875, i.e. half the 18 participants at or below the current midpoint and half above), 3.6% and 3.4%; 9 of 18 see at least one 2026 hike (6 two or more, 1 three), 8 no change, 1 a cut. The official figures are used. TE 559793 also misstates the 2027 PCE forecast (it is 2.3%).
- **Fed pricing measures differ**: Jin10 "September fully priced" (19 Jun) vs TE ~50% for September (22 Jun), ~68% "up from 29% a week ago" (23-24 Jun), FedWatch ~66% for at least one hike by September (25 Jun, July 34%), FedWatch 42% one / 28% two hikes and TE ~62% for September (26 Jun). TE 26 Jun 16:25 says "three rate hikes" are priced this year, inconsistent with FedWatch; not used.
- **BoE pricing**: the PAP week ahead (15 Jun) says a hike is fully priced by September; TE says a quarter-point is not fully priced until December. Both quoted.
- **EZ flash PMIs**: PAP has services 48.9 (May 47.7) and manufacturing 51.3 (51.6); TE's parenthesis "(47.7 vs 48.9)" is reversed and its 51.2/51.3 and Germany's 50.8 are output indices. PAP's headline PMIs are used.
- **PAP stale or bad lines (not used)**: 15 Jun 18:35 "Bund +0.6bp to 2.96%" (table 2.95%, TE two-week low; level kept, sign ignored); 23 Jun Brent $77.88 printed at both 13:55 (-0.02%) and 18:06 (-1.26%), the later level being a repeat (TE ~$77); 24 Jun 18:08 TTF "+0.06% to EUR41.90" repeats 23 Jun.
- **PAP Brent late prints**: 18 Jun 18:16 $77.44 (-2.7%) vs 22:13 $79.64 (+0.1%): an intraday swing, the late level is consistent with the 19 Jun morning print.
- **TE body issues**: 16 Jun 18:59 says the UAE "left the cartel during the conflict" (not verified, not used); re-versioned 26 Jun copies of 23 Jun items not used.
- **Iran/US narratives conflict** throughout (IAEA inspections, tolls, the Doha meeting on 30 Jun, who controls Hormuz); facts quote each side with its source.
