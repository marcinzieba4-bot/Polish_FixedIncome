# Global PIT facts 29 May - 14 Jun 2026: working notes
Windows (Warsaw publication time, CEST throughout): R0601 = 29-31 May (weekend); R0602 = 1 Jun; R0603 = 2 Jun; R0605 = 3-4 Jun (4 Jun Corpus Christi in Poland, global markets open); R0608 = 5-7 Jun (weekend); R0609 = 8 Jun; R0610 = 9 Jun; R0611 = 10 Jun; R0612 = 11 Jun; R0615 = 12-14 Jun (weekend).
Output: facts.json (166 facts + 13 fed_pricing rows), built by build/make_json.py from build/data.py (facts F and Fed-pricing rows FP, each citing one PAP/TE/Odaily item or an official URL) and build/extra.py (pricing objects, CB decisions, CPI, digests, one-liners, yield-table use notes). Schema is the May facts_global_1.json one, checked by scripts/check_schema.py (top-level keys and order, item keys and order, value types): **schema OK**.
- facts: published / window_report_date / topic / text / src; fed_pricing rows: published / text / src / window_report_date.
- fed_pricing_by_R, ecb_pricing, boe_pricing: dicts keyed by R (10 keys), short text naming the source date.
- cb_decisions: {bank, date, decision, rate, vote, published, note, src}; includes the last pre-window BoJ, Fed, BoE, ECB, CNB, NBR and MNB decisions so stances are known, plus the in-window RBI, BoC, CBRT, ECB, Danmarks Nationalbank and NBS decisions. Filter on published <= the info cut-off.
- cpi: {country, period, value, core, published, note, src}; EA, DE, FR, US (CPI and PCE), UK, CZ, HU, RO, with the previous (April) prints carried from the May files.
- digest (<= 450 chars) and glob (<= 60 chars, leading space), keyed by R.
- yield_tables: the 12 PAP/Bloomberg morning tables 29 May-15 Jun keyed by table time, with a `use` note (fact in window X / levels only for R).
The build resolves every cited PAP, Odaily and TE item from the downloads, takes the fact's time from the item when no official release time is given, and checks that the cited item's first-publication time (TE: effective time) falls in the fact's window, that every fact and CB/CPI row is published by 14 Jun 23:59, and the length limits: **no mismatches or errors remain**. Topics as in May: "NBR" = Romania (RO data), CZ data under "CNB", HU data under "MNB", UK data under "BoE"; Romanian/Hungarian/UK politics and yield tables under "sovereign"; tariffs under "geopolitics".

## Big picture (as sourced, no hindsight)
- 29-31 May: negotiators' 60-day US-Iran MoU (truce extension, Hormuz reopening, nuclear talks) awaited Trump. He posted conditions and called a Situation Room meeting for a "final decision" (29 May ~17:00) that ended without one, then sent tougher terms to Tehran (NYT, 31 May). Iran hit a US base in Kuwait; Iranian MPs pushed "sovereign jurisdiction" over Hormuz. Brent's expiring July contract fell to ~$92 (-10.5% on the week, -19% in May); Bund 10Y 2.93-2.94% (7-week low), gilt 4.81%, UST10 4.44%. German May CPI 2.6% (HICP 2.7%), French 2.4% (HICP 2.8%), Spain HICP 3.6%, Italy 3.3%. Simkus: a second ECB hike more likely than not; ~46% for a December Fed hike.
- 1-2 Jun: after weekend US "self-defence" strikes and Iranian missile fire, Iran halted talks via mediators over Israel's Lebanon strikes and threatened to fully close Hormuz (1 Jun 15:13); Brent (August) up to +7% (~$96-97), TTF EUR49; UST10 4.50%, Bund 3.00%. Trump: doesn't care if talks pause; Israel-Hezbollah halt. Euro-area May flash HICP 3.2% (core 2.5%) on 2 Jun: June ECB hike 95% priced; JOLTS 7.6m; Rubio: deal within days, no sanctions relief just for Hormuz; Trump cut some metal tariffs.
- 3-4 Jun: US strikes on Qeshm and Iranian strikes on Kuwait airport; Trump: Iran agreed not to have nuclear weapons, blockade possibly to Labor Day; Brent ~$98 then ~$95. ADP 122k, ISM services 54.5 (prices 71.3), Logan and the Beige Book lifted odds of a 2026 Fed hike to ~85%; Bloomberg: new >=10% US tariffs on 60 countries planned after the Supreme Court ruling; Czech May CPI 2.1%; Ueda opened the door to a June BoJ hike.
- 5-7 Jun: payrolls +172k (85k expected; +93k revisions) made a 25bp Fed hike by year-end almost fully priced (December 48% -> 63% in futures); UST10 >4.5%, Nasdaq's worst day in over a year, gold at a 2026 low, EUR/USD 1.153. Euro-area Q1 GDP revised to -0.2% q/q (Ireland -12.1%). Burnham announced a challenge to Starmer. Weekend: IRGC missiles at US bases, US strikes on Iranian coastal radars, Israel struck Beirut, Iran set Hormuz transit fees ($150k-2m), Trump: no advance sanctions relief; OPEC+ +188k b/d from July.
- 8 Jun: Iran fired missiles at Israel for the first time since 8 April and Israel struck Iran, until Trump got both to stop (Iran ended operations 13:19, Israel paused 14:25); Brent +4.8% to $97.6 intraday, $94.6 at the close; ~70% for a December Fed hike, ECB DFR ~2.7% by December, two BoE hikes priced; German orders -3.8%.
- 9-10 Jun: Trump said the deal was in its "final stretch", then Iran downed a US Apache over Hormuz (Mon evening; Trump post 9 Jun 19:06) and the US struck Iranian air defences overnight; Iran hit US bases in Jordan, Bahrain and Kuwait. Brent ~$91 (9 Jun) -> ~$93 (10 Jun); gold's lowest close since 10 Dec 2025. US May CPI 4.2% (core 2.9%, +0.2% m/m) in line: FedWatch June hold 96%, July hike ~10%, December still fully priced. BoC held 2.25%; Hungary May CPI 1.8%.
- 11 Jun: the ECB hiked 25bp (DFR 2.25%, unanimous; HICP 3.0% in 2026; meeting-by-meeting); next hike priced for September. Trump threatened "very hard" strikes and seizing Kharg (14:43), cancelled them (19:31) and declared a "great war settlement" (21:33); Brent -4.8% to $88.6, Wall Street +2%. US PPI +1.1% m/m; CBRT held 37%; Denmark hiked; UK Defence Secretary Healey resigned.
- 12-14 Jun: MoU terms surfaced (immediate Hormuz reopening without tolls, phased sanctions relief, 60-day truce incl. Lebanon); Iran denied finality and Trump accused it of dishonesty; Pakistan set an e-signing for the 14th; an Israeli strike on Beirut drew Ghalibaf's "dialogue impossible" (14 Jun); at 23:58 on 14 Jun Trump said the deal was reached, Hormuz open and the blockade lifted (signing 19 June in Switzerland). Brent ~$87 (-6% w/w), Bund <3%, gilt <4.8%; UK April GDP -0.1%; Romania May CPI 10.9% and a new PM nominee (Vestea).
